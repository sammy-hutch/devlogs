## GMTK Game Jam 2026: Oxygen Runner
**Project:**

[OxygenRunner](https://github.com/JonathanJegstrup/OxygenRunner)


**Details:** 

This weekend I took part in my second game jam, the GMTK Game Jam 2026. 

Last year I took part in the same jam, with the same crew as this year: [JonathanJegstrup/thenumber999](https://github.com/JonathanJegstrup) and [djent-sux/djent_sux](https://github.com/dj3nt-sux).

This year the theme was "Countdown". After many different directions in our ideation session, we settled on the concept of collecting oxygen for a colony in space. The colony's oxygen is slowly (more like quickly!) running out, so it is up to you to fly your rusty old spaceship, "The Bluebottle", out into the unknown to find the precious oxygen to keep your colony alive. On the way you can collect resources which you use to upgrade your spaceship and colony, and you discover more and more of the galaxy around you.

We developed the game over the course of 4 days (96 hours), although we were busy with work on 2 of the days. We used Godot for the game engine and Aseprite for the sprite creation.

We were super happy with the result, which included a fully playable game with working upgrade system, very immersive music and sound effects, a consistent pixelart art style and colour palette, and even a tutorial. Of course there was a lot more we wanted to include that we didn't have time for, but we were really happy that we had a working product, despite a much more ambitious scope than the previous year

The game is live on itch.io and can be played here: [oxygen-runner](https://thenumber999.itch.io/oxygen-runner)

The main takeaway for me was how good it looked when the art was kept consistent: using pixelart for everything, even the font; making sure to keep all sprites locked at their size, so no "mixels" occurred; and using a consistent colour palette with limited colours (32) to ensure consistency between sprites.

I had a lot of fun making the upgrade system, which was a bit simplistic but easy to scale and work with, and also designing the thumbnail, which took most of my time on the last day, but we felt was very important for generating interest in our game for playtesters.

We had a bit of back and forth over how much to hard-code (file paths especially) vs how much to assign in the editor, which was also a good learning for me.
